The challenge. Regulatory extraction sits at the foundation of every EPR program decision, and it is slow, high-stakes and easy to get wrong. A single misread obligation can carry through into contracts, fee structures and facility plans.

The approach. I began with the analyses I had already produced by hand: statutory citation extraction, needs assessment synthesis, public comment analysis and stakeholder mapping. I took each one apart into its underlying reasoning, then rebuilt it as a reproducible AI prompt that returns the same traceable, citation-backed output every time. Every prompt was validated by working backward from my finished manual work and checking each output against its source document. Only what held up entered the library.

The presentation. I then brought the framework to the people who would use it. I presented the methodology at an executive level to more than twenty senior company leaders and program managers, walking them through how each prompt was built, how it was verified, and how to apply it to new jurisdictions with confidence. The result was a shared, trusted method in place of one analyst’s expertise.

The following modules are generalized sample workflows demonstrating methodology. All proprietary internal documents, prompts, and strategic frameworks have been omitted for confidentiality.

How I think

Sustainability is an economic strategy, not an act of charity.

  • Goodwill is fragile. Strategy endures.

    Programs framed only as “the right thing to do” are easy to praise and just as easy to set aside.

  • Every impact has a cost. Make it visible.

    Once the impact is priced, sustainability stops being a values debate and becomes a line item worth improving.

  • Credibility is built at the source.

    I trace every number to its source, every requirement to its pinpoint citation, and every AI output to a document I can open.

  • Speak the language of the decision.

    I translate science, statute and supply chain data into ROI, risk and timelines, the terms that finance leaders, operators and program heads already use.
