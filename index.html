Engineering AI that a regulator would sign off on.

epr regulatory analysis · may–aug 2026

Regulatory extraction is slow, high-stakes and easy to get wrong. I took apart the analyst workflows I'd done by hand, then engineered them into reproducible, high-performance AI prompts that return the same traceable, citation-backed output every time. I then took the framework to leadership and trained the people who would run it.

technical rigor

  1. 01

    Dissect

    Broke down my own hand-built analyses (statutory citation extraction, needs assessment synthesis, public comment analysis, stakeholder mapping) into discrete, repeatable reasoning steps.

  2. 02

    Engineer

    Rebuilt each step as a structured prompt with strict source-only rules, explicit "not specified" handling and verbatim excerpts, so every output is auditable.

  3. 03

    Validate

    Worked backward from finished manual work and error-checked every output against the source document before a prompt entered the library.

  4. 04

    Deploy

    Packaged the prompts into a vetted, reusable library that program teams can run on new jurisdictions without starting from zero.

how i think & work

Sustainability is a business case, not a donation.

  1. 01

    Charity gets cut. Strategy gets funded.

    When sustainability is treated as goodwill, it lives in a cost center, survives on good intentions and is the first line to disappear in a tight quarter. Programs framed as "the right thing to do" are easy to praise and just as easy to defund.

  2. 02

    Every impact has a price tag. Find it.

    Material waste is margin leakage. EPR fees and ecomodulation put a number on packaging choices. Supplier emissions show up as regulatory exposure and cost of capital. Once you price the impact, sustainability stops being a values debate and becomes a line item someone wants to improve.

  3. 03

    If it can't be traced, it can't be trusted.

    An economic argument is only as strong as its weakest number. I trace every figure to its source, every requirement to its pinpoint citation, and every AI output back to a document I can open. Audit-ready isn't a finishing step; it's how the work is built.

  4. 04

    Speak the language of the person who decides.

    The best analysis does nothing if it stays in the sustainability team. I translate science, statute and supply chain data into ROI, risk and timelines: the terms that CFOs, operators and program leaders already use to make choices.
